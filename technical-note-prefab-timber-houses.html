Prefabricated modular timber single-family houses: technical basis for HVAC and plumbing installation modelling

Alinda Bhattacharjee
Compiled from the manufacturer’s 2025 technical specification · Client, product and project names withheld

Summary

This note documents the technical parameters that governed the HVAC and plumbing installation models produced for 50+ prefabricated single-family houses in the Netherlands. The houses are factory-built timber-frame dwellings with cross-laminated timber (CLT) upper floors and a prefabricated concrete ground floor, delivered to site at finished level. Ten ground-bound variants in two typologies (one-storey and two-storey, both with pitched roofs) span 80–155 m² gross floor area. All dwellings are all-electric: a heat pump supplies underfloor heating and domestic hot water via a buffer vessel, ventilation is balanced with heat recovery, and roof-integrated PV supplies on-site generation. Design targets include envelope Rc values of 6.3 / 4.7 / 4.0 m²K/W (roof / façade / floor), airtightness qv;10 ≤ 0.4 dm³/s·m², energy label A+++ or better and an environmental performance (MPG) below 0.5. The models were coordinated to Dutch NEN and ISSO requirements.

Keywords: prefabrication; modular timber housing; CLT; all-electric; heat pump; balanced ventilation with heat recovery; BENG; NEN 1010; ISSO; installation modelling; DfMA

1. Scope

The houses are designed for permanent occupation under the Dutch Building Decree for the living environment (Bbl). My work covered complete HVAC and plumbing installation models in Autodesk Revit for 50+ single-family dwellings, coordinated with the prefabricated timber elements and checked against NEN and ISSO requirements. Apartment and back-to-back variants of the same building system are outside the scope of this note.

2. Building system

  • Foundation and ground floor: insulated, self-supporting prefabricated concrete system floor with integrated edge and foundation beams, placed in segments on concrete bored or driven piles. There is no crawl space below the dwelling.
  • Upper floors: prefabricated timber-frame or CLT floor elements; the CLT underside is UV-treated and left exposed as the ceiling.
  • Load-bearing walls and roof: timber-frame construction; the pitched roof is a prefabricated insulated system roof.
  • Delivery: each house is produced off-site to finished level and transported in approximately three truckloads; on-site work after piling is limited to assembly and minor finishing.
  • Quality assurance: factory production under KOMO certification for timber-frame construction (0904), timber roof structures (0101) and assembly of prefabricated roof segments (1513-0442); timber sourced under STIP, FSC® and PEFC certification.

3. House typologies

Two ground-bound typologies are used. Type A has one full storey with bedrooms under a steep pitched roof; Type C has two full storeys plus an attic floor. Variants A4–A6 are lifetime-proof: kitchen, living room, bathroom and a bedroom are all on the ground floor. 4. Building envelope

Exterior wall (outside → inside): cladding of choice; preservative-treated battens; water-resistant membrane; spruce timber frame with insulation; PE vapour control layer; gypsum fibreboard. A windstopper layer is added where required.

Party wall (inside → outside): gypsum fibreboard; PE vapour control layer; insulated spruce timber frame; water-resistant membrane. The adjoining dwelling mirrors this build-up.

Pitched roof (outside → inside): roof covering; tile battens and counter battens; vapour-open water-resistant membrane; insulated spruce timber frame; PE vapour control layer; white or brown chipboard. Element joints are finished with cover strips.

  • Roof covering: concrete tiles as standard; ceramic tiles or sedum as options; roof and/or façade penetrations for the drainage vent stack and mechanical ventilation; PV integrated into the roof plane.
  • Roof windows with white or natural interior lining; dormers optional.
  • Window frames: PVC as standard (timber optional), opening sections with draught seals; PVC front door with letter plate; insect screens where needed; aluminium sills.
  • Glazing: HR++ insulating glass; safety, solar-control or triple glazing where required.
  • Hardware: burglary resistance class 2, keyed-alike cylinders.
  • Rainwater: aluminium eaves gutters on brackets and aluminium downpipes, optional leaf separators.

5. Interior construction

  • Partitions: non-load-bearing timber stud walls lined with gypsum fibreboard; wall thickness can vary locally where services are integrated in the walls.
  • Doors and frames: factory-finished steel frames without transom; flush lacquered doors; no thresholds except at bathroom and toilet (stone on ground floor, stainless steel upstairs); door undercuts provide the air-transfer opening required for ventilation.
  • Stairs: spruce, factory-primed white, timber balustrades and handrail.
  • Floors: ground floor smooth concrete, flatness class 4 (ready for floor covering); CLT upper floor left unfinished, suitable only for floating floor finishes, with underlay advised.
  • Ceilings: exposed CLT; dropped ceilings in toilet, bathroom and entrance/landing.
  • Walls: delivered wallpaper-ready, except the meter cupboard and technical room, which are left unfinished; meter cupboard back wall fitted with a meter board to utility requirements.
  • Service ducts: ventilation ducts and stacks are boxed in on ground and first floor, and remain exposed in the attic and unfinished loft.

7. Plumbing and sanitary

  • Bathroom: shower area, wall-hung WC, washbasin and wall mirror; walls of water-resistant board, tiled floor.
  • Separate toilet (where present): wall-hung WC and hand basin.
  • Kitchen: water, drainage and electrical connections prepared; kitchen units are optional.
  • Laundry: washing-machine connection in every dwelling (sanitary room, landing or attic); Type C also has a dryer connection in the attic.
  • Drainage: vent stack terminated through the roof; external sewer connection made on site.
  • Options with installation impact: outdoor tap on rear façade; lighting points on front and rear façade; power supply and preparation for sun shading; additional PV panels; rear extension; dormer and extra roof windows.

9. Circularity and production

Circular strategies follow the R-ladder. Whole dwellings are demountable and can be re-erected elsewhere (R3, reuse); building components are designed to be replaceable individually without replacing the dwelling (R4, repair); production residues and waste streams are minimised (R2, reduce). Bio-based alternatives are available for façade cladding, window frames, trims, roof covering (sedum), canopies and shutters, and for insulation (wood fibre, straw). Production takes place in energy-positive factories with on-site PV.

10. Implications for installation modelling

  • Ducts, pipes and cables are routed inside the prefabricated wall, floor and roof elements, so service zones, penetrations and connector positions must be fixed before factory production.
  • The exposed CLT ceilings leave no concealed zone above the upper floor; horizontal distribution relies on the dropped ceilings in the entrance, landing and sanitary rooms and on boxed-in vertical shafts.
  • Grouping the installations under the stairs in the compact variants requires tight coordination of equipment clearances and maintenance access.
  • Roof and façade penetrations for the vent stack and ventilation must be coordinated with the prefab roof elements and the integrated PV layout.
  • Airtightness (qv;10 ≤ 0.4) requires every service penetration through the vapour control layer to be detailed and sealed.
  • Optional items (outdoor tap, façade lighting, sun-shading supply, extra PV, extensions) need pre-defined provisions in the base models.
